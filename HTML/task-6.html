Before We Start: Organizing Your Files

In real-world projects, you don't just throw all your files into one big folder. You need to stay organized. Create a main folder called assets for all the images, videos, audios you would use. Inside that folder, create two subfolders: one named img (for images) and one named vid (for videos) or whatever name you like.

When you use local files from those folders, you will use relative paths (like src="assets/img/myphoto.jpg").

Part 1: The Image Tag

The Image tag is self-closing and requires an src attribute to tell the browser where the image lives, and an alt attribute for accessibility.


Part 2: Embedding YouTube Videos using Iframe

To put a YouTube video on your site, you use an Iframe. This essentially cuts a window into your webpage to display another webpage inside it.

Part 3: The HTML5 video Tag

If you are hosting your own video files (like the ones you would put in your assets/vid folder), you use the native video tag. You must include the controls attribute, or the user won't have any way to press play!
